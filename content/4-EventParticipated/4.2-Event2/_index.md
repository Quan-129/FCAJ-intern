# Event Report: Cloud Computing Competition

**Date:** 20/06/2026  
**Location:** 26th Floor, Bitexco Tower, 02 Hai Trieu Street, Saigon Ward, Ho Chi Minh City  
**Your Role:** Attendee  

---

## Brief Description of Content and Key Activities

The event was a dramatic and in-depth academic playground on cloud computing, where competing teams (such as RAM Hop, KLK, Team Newbie, and John Flame IT) faced off directly to solve AWS system architecture challenges.

**Core activities of the event included:**

1. **In-depth Multiple Choice Competition (Cloud Architecture):**
   - Teams tackled real-world case studies revolving around cost optimization, security, and performance for systems utilizing services such as EC2, S3, DynamoDB, Lambda, API Gateway, and CloudWatch.
   - Questions were divided into multiple rounds of increasing difficulty, ranging from the fundamental *Cloud Practitioner* level to *Solutions Architect Associate (SAA)* and *Professional (SAP)*.

2. **Risk Management & Competition Tactics:**
   - The competition featured highly tactical lifelines:
     - **Risk Mitigation:** Teams could choose a safe option; if correct, they received half the points, and if wrong, no points were deducted.
     - **Star of Hope:** A high-risk maneuver; if correct, points were doubled, but if wrong, points were severely deducted.
   - Teams were limited to 60 seconds to read, analyze complex problems, and provide answers, simulating the pressure of real-world decision-making.

3. **Audience Interaction & Problem Solving:**
   - The event encouraged continuous engagement by randomly inviting audience members to participate in answering. This provided an excellent opportunity for attendees to debate and analyze the most optimal system designs.

---

## Outcomes and Values Achieved

### 1. Expanding System Architecture Knowledge
- Gained a clearer awareness of how to combine various AWS resources to build highly scalable and highly available platforms.
- Deepened understanding of services like AWS Athena and AWS Macie, as well as how to implement rigorous Identity and Access Management (IAM) policies.

### 2. Practicing Decision Making under Pressure
- Observing competing teams analyze and eliminate distractor options during 60-second countdowns helped practice sharpness, information filtering, and intense focus.

### 3. Developing Critical Thinking Skills
- Debates over options A, B, C, and D provided a multi-dimensional perspective on architectural choices. I realized that there is no absolute "perfect" architecture, only the "most suitable" one based on specific cost considerations and project requirements.

### 4. Accumulating Experience & Networking
- Immersing myself in a professional academic atmosphere provided opportunities to interact and exchange knowledge with peers and seniors who share a passion for cloud computing, further solidifying my motivation for technological research.

> *"The experience at the event was not just about absorbing dry technical concepts, but more importantly, learning the mindset to approach, break down problems, and systematically handle risks."*
